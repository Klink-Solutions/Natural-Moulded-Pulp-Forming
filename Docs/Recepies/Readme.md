# Natural Moulded Pulp Forming: Recipes <sub> Subject to testing. </sub>

Four base recipes for pressing natural fibres in a 3D printed 3-part mould (base, wall, press). Every recipe is estiamted to make a part of about **100 g oven-dry**. Every amount below is **subject to testing**

## Recipe 1: Cardboard and rice starch

Baseline based largely on [^1]

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Cardboard | 97 g *(subject to testing)* |
| Cooked white rice | 33 g *(subject to testing)* |
| Water for the paste | 60 mL *(subject to testing)* |

That is about 90 g of dry fibre and 10 g of starch. *ratio is scaled from 2 tablespoons of rice paste per 30 g of paper.[1]

### Instructions

1. Weigh out the cardboard. Remove any tape, labels or glossy bits and cut it into pieces approx < 2.5 cm.[^2]
2. Soak the pieces in water for about 12 hours so the fibres separate, then drain.[^2]
3. Make the rice paste. Mash the cooked rice with the 60 mL of water in a pan, simmer and whisk until smooth, then let it cool.[^1]
4. Blend the soaked cardboard with the paste, adding small splashes of water only until it swirls and turns into a smooth mix.[^1]
5. Pre-press. Wrap the pulp in cheesecloth and squeeze out water until it feels like wet clay. Expect about 300 to 400 g of wet pulp.[^1]
6. Assemble the mould base and walls
7. Fill the mould in layers, leaving a little room at the top to push the press in level.[^1]
8. Put the whole mould in the vise or press and compress as far as it will go. To increase density stop periodically to add more pulp.[^1]
9. Leave it under pressure for about a day.[1]
10. Pry the press out slowly, because it may suction onto the pulp.[^1]
11. Leave the mould to dry for another day or more, then remove the base. The part shrinks as it dries which will loosen it from the walls.[^1]
12. Finish drying with a fan, or in an oven at 70 to 80 °C.[^2] *Oven drying needs a temperature safe plastic - not PLA.
13. Push the part out. Trim any flashing with scissors and post process if necessary.[^1]

## Recipe 2: Cardboard, alginate and calcium chloride

Recipe 1, then a gel coat on the finished part to slow down water.

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Cardboard | 97 g *(subject to testing)* |
| Cooked white rice | 33 g *(subject to testing)* |
| Water for the paste | 60 mL *(subject to testing)* |
| Sodium alginate (coating) | 5 g *(subject to testing)*[^6] |
| Glycerol (coating) | 2 g *(subject to testing)*[^6] |
| Water for the coating | 100 mL *(subject to testing)*[^6] |
| Calcium chloride | 10 g *(subject to testing)*[^6] |
| Water for the calcium bath | 200 mL, which makes 5 g per 100 mL *(subject to testing)*[^6] |

### Instructions

1. Weigh out the cardboard. Remove any tape, labels or glossy bits and cut it into pieces approx < 2.5 cm.[^2]
2. Soak the pieces in water for about 12 hours so the fibres separate, then drain.[^2]
3. Make the rice paste. Mash the cooked rice with the 60 mL of water in a pan, simmer and whisk until smooth, then let it cool.[^1]
4. Blend the soaked cardboard with the paste, adding small splashes of water only until it swirls and turns into a smooth mix.[^1]
5. Pre-press. Wrap the pulp in cheesecloth and squeeze out water until it feels like wet clay. Expect about 300 to 400 g of wet pulp.[^1]
6. Assemble the mould base and walls
7. Fill the mould in layers, leaving a little room at the top to push the press in level.[^1]
8. Put the whole mould in the vise or press and compress as far as it will go. To increase density stop periodically to add more pulp.[^1]
9. Leave it under pressure for about a day.[1]
10. Pry the press out slowly, because it may suction onto the pulp.[^1]
11. Leave the mould to dry for another day or more, then remove the base. The part shrinks as it dries which will loosen it from the walls.[^1]
12. Finish drying with a fan, or in an oven at 70 to 80 °C.[^2] *Oven drying needs a temperature safe plastic - not PLA.
13. Push the part out. Trim any flashing with scissors and post process if necessary.[^1] The part must be fully dry before coating.
14. Make the coating. Stir the alginate and glycerol into the 100 mL of water, heat at about 90 °C for 20 minutes until clear, then let cool until warm.[^6]
15. Brush one even coat over the whole part.
16. Dissolve the calcium chloride in the 200 mL of water. Dip the coated surface for 3 minutes *(subject to testing)*, then lift out and let it drain.[^6]
17. Dry the part for 24 hours at room temperature, or in an oven about 60 °C.[^6]

### Notes

- This process is meant to slow water uptake but is not waterproof.
- Don't add calcium to the pulp itself, it gels instantly and lumps.
- Calcium chloride is a mild irritant and attracts moisture. Wear gloves and eye protection, and rinse the part if it feels damp.

## Recipe 3: Leaf
100% leaf *(Test which variety works best)* with no bath. **Untested**

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Dry maple leaves | 93 g as collected, about 85 g oven-dry *(subject to testing)* |
| Cooked white rice | 50 g *(subject to testing)* |
| Water for the paste | 90 mL *(subject to testing)* |

That is about 85 g of leaf and 15 g of starch. Air-dried leaves hold about 9% moisture.[^4]

### Instructions

1. Weigh out clean, dry, fallen leaves and remove the stalks.[^4]
2. Oven dry at 70 to 80 °C until brittle (around 30 minutes) .[^5]
3. Shred the leaves with short pulses in a dry blender.
4. Soak in Initially boiling water for about 12 hours[^2] (Pouring hot water on should help the waxy surface wet easier).
5. Make the rice paste. Mash the cooked rice with the 60 mL of water in a pan, simmer and whisk until smooth, then let it cool.[^1]
6. Blend the soaked leaves with the paste, adding small splashes of water only until it swirls and turns into a smooth mix.[^1]
7. Pre-press. Wrap the pulp in cheesecloth and squeeze out water until it feels like wet clay.
8. Assemble the mould base and walls
9. Fill the mould in layers, leaving a little room at the top to push the press in level.[^1]
10. Put the whole mould in the vise or press and compress as far as it will go. To increase density stop periodically to add more pulp.[^1]
11. Leave it under pressure for about a day.[^1]
12. Pry the press out slowly, because it may suction onto the pulp.[^1]
13. Leave the mould to dry for another day or more, then remove the base. The part shrinks as it dries which will loosen it from the walls.[^1]
14. Finish drying with a fan, or in an oven at 70 to 80 °C.[^2] *Oven drying needs a temperature safe plastic - not PLA.
15. Push the part out. Trim any flashing with scissors and post process if necessary.[^1]

### Notes

- Maple leaf is only about 15% cellulose, and 100% leaf paper was very weak in the maple leaf study,[^4] so the starch does most of the bonding here.
- Studies have found more success with pre treated leaves which we can attempt small scale below

## Recipe 4: Leaf+
100% maple leaf, treated in a mild baking soda bath before pressing.

| Ingredient | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Dry maple leaves | 126 g as collected, about 115 g oven-dry *(subject to testing)* |
| Water for the bath | 2.3 L *(subject to testing)* |
| Baking soda | 23 g *(subject to testing)* |
| Cooked white rice | 50 g *(subject to testing)* |
| Water for the paste | 90 mL *(subject to testing)* |

That is about 85 g of treated leaf and 15 g of starch
### Instructions

1. Weigh out the leaves. Remove the stalks, tear the leaves into small pieces and rinse off any grit.[^4]
2. Dissolve the baking soda in the water in a pan that is not used for food. Add the leaves and hold at 80 to 90 °C, below a hard boil, for 60 minutes, stirring now and then. The water will go dark brown. *(The temperature and time are based maple leaf study, which used different,more agitating chemicals)[^4]
3. Drain through a fine sieve and rinse until the water runs pale.
4.Make the rice paste. Mash the cooked rice with the 60 mL of water in a pan, simmer and whisk until smooth, then let it cool.[^1]
5. Blend the soaked leaves with the paste, adding small splashes of water only until it swirls and turns into a smooth mix.[^1]
6. Pre-press. Wrap the pulp in cheesecloth and squeeze out water until it feels like wet clay.
7. Assemble the mould base and walls
8. Fill the mould in layers, leaving a little room at the top to push the press in level.[^1]
9. Put the whole mould in the vise or press and compress as far as it will go. To increase density stop periodically to add more pulp.[^1]
10. Leave it under pressure for about a day.[1]
11. Pry the press out slowly, because it may suction onto the pulp.[^1]
12. Leave the mould to dry for another day or more, then remove the base. The part shrinks as it dries which will loosen it from the walls.[^1]
13. Finish drying with a fan, or in an oven at 70 to 80 °C.[^2] *Oven drying needs a temperature safe plastic - not PLA.
14. Push the part out. Trim any flashing with scissors and post process if necessary.[^1]

### Notes

- Baking soda is a weak base. Real leaf pulping used 16% sodium hydroxide, sodium sulphide and anthraquinone for 2 hours at 100 °C.[^5] 
- Lignin acts as a binder mainly under heat, for example around 160 °C and 0.5 MPa, so heat pressing may be required. [^3]
- Fine leaf bits may clog drain holes.
- Leaf tableware broke down in soil within 5 to 14 days, so a wet leaf part may not last long outdoors.[^5]
